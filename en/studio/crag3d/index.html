Turn a 2D guidebook into 3D

For crags that already have a 2D guidebook: shoot one round of photos, and AI and tools place the routes on the 3D wall. In Redpoint, climbers stand on the trail, see the whole wall, walk along it and tap routes.

How to shoot See the workflow

What you get

1 · Walk along the wall

Like walking the trail

Tap a crag to go straight into 3D. See the whole crag at once and drag left or right to walk along the wall, not area by area.

2 · Routes on the wall

Numbers, grades, notes

Your guidebook’s numbers, grades and notes carry over. Tap a route and the camera walks to it and shows its card. Routes hidden behind rock are not drawn.

3 · Offline on the phone

One download

Like any other crag: one download, readable offline. Works on phones and computers.

  • Your existing guidebook is used as is. 3D is one more way to look at it; crags without 3D stay as 2D topos.
  • AI finds where each route sits in 3D and tools check and score it; someone still looks before you publish.
  • Built only from photos: AI never guesses to fill in rock nobody photographed. Shoot that part and it is filled in.

Workflow

Six steps. Each says who does what, then lists what can go wrong.

Step 1 · Start from a 2D guidebook

Get the guidebook ready

You Prepare it in Studio with Convert a guidebook: walls, routes and photos, with the routes drawn on the 2D photos. The 3D version adds a model to this book; route numbers, grades and notes carry over.

Step 2 · Shoot

Two sets: close-up, plus step-back

You Shoot two sets for the same crag: close-up (builds the near wall and recognises start holds) and step-back (so readers standing on the trail see the whole wall without fog). See How to shoot 3D photos. No need to pick photos or mark routes on site.

What can go wrong
  • Only close-ups: it builds, but the whole-wall view from the trail turns foggy with ghosting at the top. Add a step-back set.
Step 3 · Build the 3D model

Automatic: build, trim, shrink

Auto Sorting photos, filtering the useless ones, building the model, trimming stray edges and clutter, and shrinking it to fit a phone. Take a look for fog or holes; if none, move on.

What can go wrong
  • A patch looks foggy: no photos from that angle. Go back and shoot that side (step back, arc, one pass each at high, mid and low).
  • Holes or a missing piece: usually hidden or unphotographed. Shoot that side.
  • Bushes in front of the wall: adjust the camera position, or shoot at a different height.
Step 4 · Place the routes in 3D

AI uses your 2D routes to find positions

Auto AI reads the routes drawn on your guidebook photos and finds the matching spot on the 3D wall. Inside corners and arêtes look flat head-on, so it projects from each old photo’s own viewpoint onto the model. Tools then score each route (overlap when projected back onto the old photo, agreement across photos, sitting on the rock surface, reaching the top) and align the starts of neighbouring routes. Take a look only at the low scores.

What can go wrong
  • A line floats in the air or sits on the neighbouring block: low scores are picked out; drag the line back onto the rock.
  • An inside-corner line is on the wrong face: compare the left and right 35° views with the old photo.
  • Starts at different heights: neighbouring starts are aligned automatically; fix any that are still off by hand.
Step 5 · Check in Redpoint

Walk the wall once

Take a look Walk along the wall and check each line’s position, number, grade, start and finish. Lines hidden behind rock are not drawn and appear as you get closer; that is normal, not missing.

Step 6 · Publish

Credit and licence follow the guidebook

You confirm the credit; public content is CC BY-SA 4.0 like other public crags. Once published to the open dataset, Redpoint receives the 3D version (books without a 3D model stay 2D).

FAQ

Do I have to shoot both sets?

To just try it, close-ups alone build a model, but readers looking at the whole wall from the trail see fog. For the feel of walking the crag, the step-back set is needed.

Are the files big?

The Dapaoyan model is about 24 MB, plus a snapshot of about 0.5 MB per wall. Redpoint downloads once and works offline; model size is kept within what a phone can hold.

What about crags without 3D?

Unchanged: wall cards on the crag page and 2D topos.

What do I do after shooting?

Hand the photos to Climbio to build it together; once Studio’s 3D screens are ready, you will do it in Studio yourself.
