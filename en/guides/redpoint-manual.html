Red Dot Cat guide

Take wherever you want to climb with you. Red Dot Cat is a guidebook reader; to edit and produce guidebooks, use Studio.

Open Red Dot Cat

First time

  1. Open: open Red Dot Cat in your phone browser. No sign-in or install needed.
  2. Get a crag: tap the globe, World, at the bottom right, find a crag on the map and download its guidebook.
  3. Check: open the crag and its topos and confirm all images are downloaded; before you leave, you can check in airplane mode.

Install

Red Dot Cat is a web app (PWA); no app store needed. After you first get a crag, the home screen shows a one-time, dismissible Add to Home Screen prompt. If you visit the same crags often and need long-term offline use, install first and then download.

iPhone / iPad

Open Red Dot Cat in Safari, tap Share, then Add to Home Screen.

Android

Open Red Dot Cat in Chrome and tap Install or Add to Home screen.

Links from LINE, Messenger and Instagram can be received right in the original browser; you only need Safari or Chrome to add to the home screen.

On iPhone, the browser and the home-screen app keep separate local data, so a newly installed home-screen app may need to download crags again.

Downloaded crags, topos, climb logs and local backup files work without signing in. Publishing public content may require verifying the publisher.

Find crags

  1. On the home screen, tap the globe, World, at the bottom right.
  2. World opens as stacks of country cards, arranged roughly by geography; a thicker stack means more crags.
  3. Tap a stack to open its regions, then a region to spread it into crag cards; Back at the top left folds one level.
  4. Pick a crag, check the available guidebooks and get one.

World includes guidebooks that authors published through Climbio and available OpenClimbing data. It is not a complete directory of crags, and not every point has a topo. If you cannot find one, use a friend's share link, collection tool or a local data pack import.

Offline download

  1. In World, pick a crag and tap Download or Get, or open a guidebook link someone sent you.
  2. Wait for photos, routes and map data to finish downloading.
  3. Tap View this crag; the crag also appears on the home screen.
  4. Before you leave, open a few topos and confirm there is no Photo not downloaded notice.

Data is stored in the browser or app storage on this device. Without a connection you can still view fully downloaded crags, topos and your own log.

Opening World the first time, downloading new crags and using external navigation need a connection. If a download is interrupted, finish the photos when you are back online by following the prompt.

Browse a crag

  1. On the home screen, tap a crag card to open the crag page (entrance photo and a small card for each wall).
  2. Tap a wall card to open its first topo.
  3. With several topos, swipe left and right; you can also choose another wall from the crag page.
  4. Tap the paw at the bottom right to open the route list.

Data varies by source; buttons for access, original data or area maps appear next to the entrance photo only when that data exists.

Topo and 3D

A topo is a route diagram laid over a wall photo, showing lines, numbers and grades according to the data.

Missing information (bolts, anchors and so on) is never filled in.

3D walls

Some guidebooks include a 3D model; open the model photo and use gestures to rotate the view. Crags without a model still show photo topos as usual.

Maps, access and navigation

These entries appear on the crag page only when the data provides them.

  • Crag map: shows walls, boulders, sectors and trails, and links to the matching topo.
  • Area photos / layout: shows how the walls are laid out in photos.
  • Access notes: parking, entrance and trail information supplied by the author.
  • Navigation: when coordinates exist, opens an external map app to get there.

A map position is only a reference point for the crag or its entrance, not necessarily the start of a route. It does not replace judgment on site, legal access information or weather assessment. Whether an external map works offline depends on that app.

Climb log

  1. On the crag page, tap the paw at the bottom right to open the route list (organized by wall).
  2. Tap a route to jump back to its topo with the route highlighted.
  3. Tap the paw status at the right of a route to cycle Not climbed, Sent, Climbed, Not climbed.
  4. At the top of the list, filter by sent, climbed or not climbed; tap the current filter again to clear it.

The log is stored on this device and is not synced to other phones by Google sign-in; back up before switching phones.

Organize and merge crags

On the home screen, long-press a card until it lifts, then drag:

  • Drop between two cards: reorder.
  • Drop onto another crag card: merge into a collection (a single topo card cannot be merged).
  • Long-press and release: actions such as Share and Delete appear; a collection can be renamed or split.

After merging, the original data, sources and your own log are kept and can be split again. If Auto-merge crags is in Settings, it lists candidates based on how the sources relate, for you to confirm.

Delete removes the crag and its photos from this device, and deleting a collection deletes its members too; without a backup it may not be recoverable. Deleting locally does not affect the original source or backup files you saved elsewhere.

Collect and import

Method A: share link

Open a Red Dot Cat or Climbio guidebook link a friend sent and get it. If you already have it, the local copy opens directly; whether it works depends on the original data's permissions and your connection.

Method B: collect external sites in desktop Chrome

  1. Install the Red Dot Cat collection tool in desktop Chrome.
  2. Go to a supported site (theCrag or OpenClimbing pages with topos, or sites that provide the Climbio format), open the crag page and click the collection tool.
  3. The extension opens Red Dot Cat on your computer and hands the crag over (no sign-in).
  4. Connect the computer and phone to the same network, then open Avatar → Sync to other devices in Red Dot Cat on both and pair them once. Your personal collection is then synchronized between your devices; Nearby exchange is for sharing permitted content with other people.

This is a desktop flow that supports only some sites and does not guarantee a topo for every route; phone browsers do not have the same collection feature.

Method C: local file

Settings → Import, then choose a supported .climbio or ZIP data pack. If the file holds several crags, pick only the ones you want.

A personal collection is not a public guidebook. Data from other sites is bound by its original license, is usually for your own offline reading, does not appear in World, and cannot be shared directly.

Share

On a crag or topo you have permission to share, tap the share button to send a link; the recipient opens it to add it to their own Red Dot Cat.

  • A public guidebook you received: you can forward the original link; if you share a specific topo, the recipient sees that photo directly after getting it.
  • Content you made and have the right to publish: until it goes live, publish with Climbio Studio (publishing is always public); to give it to specific people use an export file or nearby exchange.
  • Personal collections of external sites: no share permission, so no share action is shown.
  • Collections: cannot be shared as a group; share from a member or topo you have permission for.

Private means anyone with the link can get it; public may also be listed in the World index. Before going public, check the text, topos, photos and each author's license.

Send to others (Nearby transfer)

  1. Connect to the same Wi-Fi that allows devices to talk directly. In Red Dot Cat, tap the nearby-transfer icon at the top right of World on both devices.
  2. Select nearby people and the shareable crags or topos you want to send. You can choose several recipients.
  3. Each recipient taps Accept before the content is saved locally. No pairing is required.

Personal collections and climb logs are not offered to other people. Receiving content this way does not publish it in World.

Guest Wi-Fi can block direct connections. QR-code exchange is available only if the discovery service is unconfigured or unreachable.

My devices (two-way sync)

  1. On your computer, phone or tablet, open Avatar → Sync to other devices → Add new device on both devices.
  2. Choose the peer, compare the four-digit codes, and tap Confirm on both screens.
  3. Once paired, missing or newer crags, topos, personal collections and climb logs sync automatically. The page shows the current sync status.

Pair once. On later visits, open My devices on both devices while they can connect directly on the same network to sync again. Nothing syncs merely because the app is open in the background.

Deletions do not sync, and climb records only accumulate. Use a local export/import backup when direct connections are not possible. Red Dot Cat and Hold Cat share a nickname but require separate pairings.

Backup and switching phones

Crags, photos, topos and your climb log live mainly on the device. Use a local .climbio backup or pair your devices on the same network. Export a backup before switching phones.

Export

Settings, Export, choose crags, then save a local .climbio file. It includes the photos, topos and climb log of the selected crags.

Import

Settings, Import, select a local .climbio or ZIP file, then choose the crags to restore. If the same data already exists, read the overwrite notice first.

Export creates a local .climbio backup file without sign-in. Import restores selected crags; paired devices can also exchange personal data while both are online on the same network.

To switch phones: export on the old phone, confirm it finished, install Red Dot Cat on the new phone and import, then check photos and your log. Do not clear the old device until the restore succeeds.

Updates, connection and storage

  • Downloaded first: the app shows local data first, without waiting for the network.
  • New version check: when you open a saved crag online and the source has a new version, it updates; not every import source supports this.
  • Lost signal: when a notice says some photos are not downloaded, finish them when you are online. Text and routes without photos are not a complete offline topo.
  • Phone storage: photos and large data packs take space. Clearing site data, switching browsers or removing the app may erase local data; export important records first.
  • Outdoors: before you leave, open the walls you need and check source and access information; a map is not a safety guarantee.

FAQ

I can't find the crag I want in World

World includes only the data currently available. Try panning or zooming the map, using a share link, the desktop collection tool, or importing a local data pack.

Photos do not show up in the mountains

The download may be incomplete, some photo sources may have failed, or it was downloaded in a different browser environment. When you are online again, finish the download in the same Red Dot Cat app by following the prompt, then check in airplane mode.

Can I collect from theCrag directly on my phone?

Use the desktop Chrome extension to collect a crag into Red Dot Cat, then pair computer and phone under Avatar → Sync to other devices while they are on the same network.

Why does a crag have no topo lines, map or 3D?

Red Dot Cat shows only what the source provides: some crags have only a route list, some photos have no lines attached, and 3D, maps and navigation need a model or location data.

Why does my other phone not have the same crags?

Use a local backup and restore, reopen the original share link, or pair your own devices on the same network. Climb logs are included in backups or paired-device sync.

Why does a personal collection have no share button?

A collection only means you may read it yourself, not that you have the right to republish it. See Sources and licenses, and contact the author if needed.

Can I change routes or draw topos in Red Dot Cat?

No. To make or edit a guidebook use Studio; if you already have a guidebook, see the guide to converting guidebooks.
